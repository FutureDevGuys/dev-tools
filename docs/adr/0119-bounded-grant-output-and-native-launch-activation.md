# ADR 0119: Bounded grant output and native launch activation

status: proposed
verification: pending

## Decision

Complete the remaining execution source contract from ADR0115 through explicit plan output limits, ordinary launch-with-grant, and maintained direct administrator aliases. Existing bounded maintenance `plan/request/execute/execute-plan/status/revoke` grammar, policy and generator semantics remain unchanged. `dev-auth privilege inspect|exec|delegate|leave` are exact aliases of the corresponding `privilege grant` commands and share one typed Clap leaf builder. Administrator `request/status/revoke` retain their explicit `privilege grant` namespace; no policy-shape inference selects authority. Private launch activation, graph and output-relay operations are absent from public completion and cannot enter through the enrolled set-ID dispatcher.

## Exact plan output contract

An `ExecutionPlan` may select `output_limit_bytes` as an integer from 1 through 16777216. Absent/null values normalize to the preserved absent default and serialize without a new field, preserving existing plan approval bytes. A selected integer participates in the exact canonical plan digest. The upper bound remains within JCS's exact integer range; floating values, zero, overflowing or larger limits are rejected. A capped plan must execute through its selected graph (`--operation` or `--plan-sha256`); matching argv or native Sudo cannot bypass capture. Unrestricted native Sudo and uncapped execution retain their explicitly selected native semantics.

The limit aggregates externally forwarded stdout, stderr and merged PTY output for one owned job. PTY bytes are counted once, alongside any separately redirected channel; terminal input and graph-internal pipe edges are excluded. The relay preserves native terminal/run-as behavior and the caller's open-file-description flags, offsets and held sink descriptors. A root-only, held installed-core relay inside that job owns nonblocking source pipes/PTY, bounded queues and separate blocking sink writers. Its main reader can detect the remaining budget plus one byte while a sink writer is blocked. Aggregate retained payload is bounded by the approved budget plus fixed buffers. The relay retains the actual owned worker's pidfd and starts one bounded five-second drain after worker exit or source EOF, including when the coordinator is stopped. Sink writer joins are polled before joining; drain cannot refresh its deadline. Trusted transport/graph helpers survive forwarded workload signals, while payloads restore their original signal mask and worker job-control stops remain observable. It closes only that job's held admission gate and cgroup on overflow or transport failure, preserving other grants and the ordinary non-root workload.

A fixed-length private memfd frame contains only counters and sticky failure/completion state. Its layout is explicitly 80 bytes with eight-byte alignment and lock-free 64-bit atomics on Linux x86_64/aarch64; other targets fail closed. Length seals prevent invalidating mapped storage. One designated reader owns observed slots, one writer owns each forwarded slot, and all shared accesses are atomic. No payload enters this frame. Public outcomes report bounded observed counts (at most the cap plus one detector byte), forwarded counts, limit excess and completion. Incomplete counters are confirmed lower bounds; exact completion requires every output stream, writer, both direct child owners, and positive cgroup teardown to terminalize. Overflow, relay failure or failed bounded cleanup cannot publish success. The coordinator retains both Child owners for later cleanup when a caller disconnects or settlement is uncertain.

## Launch activation and native proof

`dev-auth workload launch NAME --privilege-scope user|workload --privilege-duration DURATION -- ...` explicitly selects fresh unrestricted administrator approval within policy. Scope and duration must occur together; no idle timeout/use budget is silently added. Noninteractive use returns `approval_required` before setup, authorization or child admission. Strong installed workload admission remains independently required; enrolled admission does not authorize a fresh administrator request through its set-ID surface. The request travels separately before the private workload argv separator.

The authenticated installed supervisor requests a pending grant. The coordinator retains the requester's native pidfd, registered cgroup/scope and root-owned installed ELF identity. Pending grants publish no routing marker and cannot execute or delegate; an abandoned owner is revoked. Activation requires that same live retained native owner, unchanged policy/setup authority and the original unexpired lease. Activation never renews the approval-time deadline.

The configured target reaches a kernel final-exec stop before executing target instructions. The owning monitor arms `SIGKILL` parent-death enforcement and checks its actual parent plus retained parent pidfd before `PTRACE_TRACEME`, covering death before EXITKILL is installed. It uses non-reaping `waitid` observation of its owned child, `PTRACE_O_EXITKILL` custody and `PTRACE_DETACH` only after successful activation. `Command::spawn` observes its exec-error pipe; no blocking pre-exec gate deadlocks that spawn. A paired private proof socket and root-owned release pipe keep strong and sandbox target startup stopped while the coordinator authenticates readiness and activates the grant. Nested execution uses an additive consumed-command post-spawn callback with a borrowed Child; exclusive reaping, signal/group custody, bounded cleanup and existing nested deadlines remain in the current runner. Failed exec, proof, activation or release stops only the owned startup child and revokes the pending/new grant. Startup/release descriptors are paired typed owners and close at the final target exec. The complete prepared command, including owned executable/source callbacks, survives monitor composition. Installed-core monitors protect retained descriptors with a nondumpable process state.


Readiness uses a Unix seqpacket socket configured for `SO_PASSCRED` and `SO_PASSPIDFD`. The privileged coordinator receives one exact readiness byte, kernel `SCM_CREDENTIALS` and its atomic `SCM_PIDFD`, and rejects missing, truncated, duplicate or unexpected ancillary data. Every delivered descriptor is owned and rejected rights close. The caller transfers exactly the proof endpoint, its retained actual spawn-owner pidfd and the private nonblocking release writer in an explicit private bootstrap activation; no submitted numeric PID is an authority. The coordinator proves that spawn's native parent is the retained pending LaunchOwner caller, then requires the sender to be that live spawn or a bounded retained native descendant. Sender identity, actual cgroup and exact current root-owned installed-core image are rechecked immediately before publication. Same UID plus the same binary, cached socketpair creator credentials, and a borrowed endpoint cannot substitute for that retained lineage. The receiver runs in the privileged coordinator so descriptor nondumpability does not prevent current-image checks.


Routing availability and successful release commit are distinct. After final-exec readiness, the coordinator publishes an available grant while retaining its installed caller's native pidfd, exact image and cgroup in a release phase. Admission and delegation check that live release owner (including ancestors), so the target's first Sudo is available before commit without granting authority after owner loss. The coordinator owns and writes the bootstrap release endpoint, then requires an exact kernel-authenticated witness from the retained monitor after successful `PTRACE_DETACH`. The monitor keeps its protected proof socket and waits for a coordinator commit receipt before waiting for/exiting with the target; immediate target completion cannot destroy sender identity before witness authentication. Only that witness, unchanged live authority and the current lease clear temporary release custody. Nested owned-child execution similarly sends an authenticated private commit only after its owning callback successfully detaches the native stop. No failed or repeated activation revives a stopped lease or extends its deadline.

Controller or monitor death/failure before commit withdraws the uncommitted routing marker, closes its grant boundary and stops delegated descendants. Periodic reaping and every new request sweep abandoned release custody; admission separately denies a dead or changed release owner. A fixed fifteen-second BOOTTIME deadline bounds the release phase even if a coordinator task is cancelled while the caller stays alive. Successful withdrawal removes startup-only ownership while registry/native boundary state retains cleanup custody; successful user-scope commit retains its independently approved lifetime. Release/message writes are nonblocking private transports, and commit is never approximated by sleeping, replaying a request or checking that the target remains alive.

This proof ABI is explicitly limited to Linux x86_64/aarch64 with atomic sender-pidfd socket support; unsupported options or missing kernel sender custody fail closed before configured target release. The constants and receive-only pidfd control message are recorded in the fixed [Linux v6.12 socket UAPI](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.12/include/uapi/asm-generic/socket.h) and [kernel socket definitions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.12/include/linux/socket.h). The configured target stays at its final native exec stop throughout proof validation and marker activation. Failed readiness, expiry, activation, sender death or release closes the gate and revokes without extending the grant deadline.

The Linux [ptrace manual](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/ptrace.2.html) specifies the pre-instruction exec stop and detach; EXITKILL takes effect only after its option is set. The [parent-death signal manual](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/PR_SET_PDEATHSIG.2const.html) documents signal clearing on credential and set-ID/file-capability transitions. The pre-EXITKILL interval therefore uses an uncatchable SIGKILL plus the immediate retained parent-identity check, and the configured target cannot introduce one of those exec transitions. Tracing also changes set-ID/file-capability exec semantics, so this grant launch path rejects those configured launcher identities rather than silently changing execution. Native Sudo invoked after activation/detach retains its normal parsing, environment, run-as and credential semantics. Missing or restricted native tracing fails closed; it is never approximated with a post-spawn timing assumption or a submitted PID hint. Credential/logical-v3 authority, SSH/provider metadata, workload diagnostics and their independently retained hard deadlines remain authoritative.


A sandbox adapter declares an optional `inherited_descriptor_arguments` list in administrator policy: at most eight literal arguments of 256 bytes each, with exactly one `{count}` placeholder. The installed child repeats native broker admission and recomputes the current selected adapter, requiring the transported executable, fixed arguments, separator and namespace contract to match. Only the current policy supplies descriptor preservation. Empty legacy declarations preserve ordinary descriptor-free behavior and fail closed when private descriptors are required; no provider flags or shell fragments are guessed.

One typed product-owned descriptor set has at most three owners in proof, release, optional verified shell-source order. It retains sources at FD 6 or above and maps compact slots 3 through 5 only when an installed intermediate calls `CommandExt::exec`. Mapping never occurs under `Command::spawn`, whose newly allocated exec-error pipe could otherwise be overwritten and invalidate final-exec proof. The private intermediate is single-threaded during mapping. Every occupied compact slot is backed up with its original open-file-description and descriptor flags; failed pre-exec/exec restores it before existing Rust owners resume, while previously closed destinations close. Restoration failure terminates the private intermediate. The owning guard remains live across exec. Binding composition extends this same set with its independently verified sealed shell source and retains a configured executable outside compact slots; it does not replace the prepared-command startup barrier.

## Qualification and composition

For a strong launch with terminal stdin and redirected output, systemd preserves
the selected streams through its pipe mode, which provides no controlling terminal
inside the service. The existing root dispatcher retains the request-owned native
PolicyKit terminal agent on its real foreground terminal, registered for the
authenticated inner supervisor's PID and start time. Its held pidfd, current
cgroup and installed executable are checked around agent readiness before the
existing environment handoff releases that supervisor. This agent supplies UI
only; the coordinator still authenticates the request and requires fresh approval.
The inner client declines a local terminal agent when `/dev/tty` reports no
controlling terminal. All-terminal and noninteractive launches retain their
existing agent selection. No stream, approval or grant-lifetime contract changes.

Source fixtures cover aliases, unchanged maintenance grammar/default bytes, exact cap digests/bounds, aggregate overflow, channel separation, blocked sinks, descriptor flags and failed-exec restoration, bounded drain settlement, disconnects, atomic foreign-sender rejection during the monitor entry window, rejected ancillary-rights cleanup, user-controller death after routing publication but before release commit, native spawn proof and a target marker that cannot appear before release. Ignored native fixtures target actual owned cgroup cleanup with a blocked sink and an unaffected sibling. These definitions and formatting do not establish runtime support.

Required native acceptance includes both launch scopes with real fresh approval, first-instruction Sudo after activation, failed/missing-interpreter startup, activation expiry/failure, stopped/dead controller or monitor including after activation publication before release witness/commit, immediate target exit and first-instruction Sudo, sandbox descriptor custody and authenticated native descendant proof, per-message sender-pidfd capability failure, cap/terminal/run-as behavior, blocked/disconnected sinks, positive job teardown and preserved unrelated grants/non-root work. Signed setup/helper payload, interruption/recovery/rollback/removal, binding/source/pinned-shell composition and setup-aware updates remain separate qualification slices. ADR0115/0116 maintenance and setup retention contracts remain in force. No other platform is qualified by this source change.
